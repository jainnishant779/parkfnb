# Technical Limitations and Mitigations for Single 2D-Camera Parking Occupancy Systems (as of 2025-2026)

## What are the well-documented failure modes of single 2D camera parking occupancy systems (occlusion, orientation errors, lighting, weather, perspective/homography distortion)?

### Takeaway
Single fixed-camera parking occupancy systems are consistently reported to degrade under five recurring conditions — inter-vehicle/structural occlusion, oblique/angled viewing geometry, extreme or changing illumination (night, glare, shadow), adverse weather (rain, fog, snow), and perspective foreshortening for spots far from the camera — with published accuracy drops in the range of ~9–24 percentage points versus clean-condition baselines depending on the failure mode.

### Cited Findings
- Single-camera parking systems suffer from occlusions, blind spots, and sensitivity to environmental conditions; background occlusions from trees, streetlights, or traffic lights partially block several parking spaces at once because one camera must cover many slots — [Advanced IoT-integrated parking systems](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11742719/)
- Lens distortion is common "when a single camera is in charge of several slots," and dramatic illumination changes occur from over/underexposure since systems must run 24 hours a day — [Advanced IoT-integrated parking systems](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11742719/)
- Real-world evaluation images with "strong shadows, occlusions, faded markings, and oblique camera angles" produce lower model confidence (~0.60) versus clean conditions, and oblique/aerial imaging causes "large variations in vehicle target scales" — [DJI Mini 3 + YOLOv11 occupancy study, MDPI Drones 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-2688/7/2/74)
- On a large-scale real-world parking-slot dataset (CRPS-D, 35,379 images across 8 scene categories), documented failure modes of prior detectors include: complex underground lighting with reflections causing false positives, outdoor glare impairing detection, heavy rain reducing contrast and obscuring markings, faded/damaged lines causing missed detections, vehicle occlusion blocking slot views, and nighttime scenes with "limited lighting and sensor noise" — [Advancing Real-World Parking Slot Detection with Large-Scale Dataset and Semi-Supervised Baseline, arXiv 2509.13133](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13133v1)
- CRPS-D deliberately over-represents slanted/angled parking slots (11.75% of the dataset) versus the older ps2.0 benchmark (3.41%), reflecting that angled parking is both more realistic and harder for detectors — [arXiv 2509.13133](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13133v1)
- On this harder, more realistic dataset, a strong fully-supervised baseline (DMPR-PS) only reaches 87.20% AP on CRPS-D vs. 98.64% AP on the easier/older ps2.0 dataset — the same architecture, ~11.4 point drop, purely from harder real-world conditions (occlusion, lighting, damaged markings, slant) — [arXiv 2509.13133](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13133v1)
- A hazy-conditions parking study found foggy conditions gave the lowest detection accuracy of any weather condition tested — 85.7% on weekdays and 91.6% on weekends, with thicker fog further reducing accuracy — [Smart Parking Space Detection under Hazy Conditions, arXiv 2201.05858](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.05858) (via search synthesis; original claim, not independently re-verified from full text)
- A separate performance breakdown reported specific accuracy drops by condition: Outdoor Night −23.23 points, Outdoor Rainy −15.61 points, Damaged markings −14.85 points, relative to baseline/clean conditions — [search synthesis, source: parking occupancy weather robustness literature](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13133v1)
- Bounding-box change in size is "not necessarily related to object-to-camera distance due to camera distortion or even object occlusion" — a core reason bounding-box-only pipelines fail geometrically for footprint/occupancy estimation, especially at range — [Voxel51: Image Segmentation Maps vs Bounding Boxes](https://voxel51.com/blog/why-are-image-segmentation-maps-superior-to-bounding-boxes)
- IPM/IPM-based homography-to-BEV projection assumes a planar world and that camera horizontal axes are parallel to the ground; it requires accurate camera intrinsics/extrinsics and "is likely to produce inferior view transformation," suffering "blurring and stretching artifacts for distant objects and objects with height" — this directly explains footprint distortion for far-from-camera or elevated (i.e., tall vehicle) targets — [Emergent Mind: Camera-to-BEV Projection Techniques](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/camera-to-bev-projection); [Emergent Mind: Inverse Perspective Mapping](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/inverse-perspective-mapping-ipm)
- Homography/IPM accuracy for parking-spot recognition "depends on the calibration accuracy of the camera and the homography calibration," and real-world performance is "degraded by distortion errors resulting from inaccurate camera calibration"; distant, low-resolution vehicles have "insufficient geometric detail for accurate regression" — [search synthesis of homography/perspective-distortion literature](https://doi.org/10.3390/s25061891)

### Inferences
- The failure modes are not independent: oblique-angle mounting (needed to reduce occlusion, see camera-placement section below) directly increases homography/perspective distortion for far slots, so a single fixed 2D camera cannot simultaneously minimize both occlusion and geometric distortion — this is a structural trade-off, not a tuning problem.
- The consistent ~10-25 point accuracy gap between "clean" published benchmarks (PKLot/CNRPark, ps2.0) and newer "realistic" datasets (CRPS-D) suggests older headline accuracy numbers (97-99%) are not representative of field performance in angled/occluded/adverse-weather deployments — engineers should treat 97%+ PKLot/CNRPark numbers as a ceiling, not an expectation.

## What are standard industry/academic mitigation techniques (multi-camera fusion, BEV/homography refinement, temporal tracking, segmentation vs bounding boxes)?

### Takeaway
The literature converges on four complementary mitigation layers: (1) multi-camera overlap to physically eliminate blind spots, (2) deep-learning-refined BEV/IPM (GAN or learned-decoder correction) instead of raw geometric homography, (3) temporal multi-object tracking (Kalman filter + Hungarian/DeepSORT-style re-association) to bridge momentary occlusion using motion history, and (4) polygon/instance-segmentation representations instead of axis-aligned bounding boxes for tighter, occlusion-robust footprint estimation.

### Cited Findings

**Multi-camera fusion**
- Ensuring "at least 10–20% overlap between camera coverage avoids blind spots and maintains continuous coverage" is a standard CCTV/ITS design recommendation for parking lots — [CCTV Camera Placement Guide 2026](https://cctvplanner.io/blog/camera-placement-guide)
- "Occlusion in overlapping fields of view between camera pairs results in less impact compared to other occlusion types" — i.e., overlap specifically mitigates occlusion better than other fixes — [Minimizing Occlusion Effect on Multi-View Camera Perception in BEV with Multi-Sensor Fusion, arXiv 2501.05997](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05997v1)
- The same study found that "realistic camera occlusion degrades bird's-eye view vehicle segmentation more than random or overlap occlusion," but this degradation "is systematically mitigated by adding radar and LiDAR sensors" — implying camera-only multi-view fusion helps but has a ceiling; heterogeneous sensor fusion goes further — [arXiv 2501.05997](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05997v1)
- 2024 research on multi-camera multi-vehicle tracking with overlapping FoVs on highways addresses "target scale variation... in near, medium and far fields of view" by categorizing vehicles by confidence level based on occlusion severity — a technique transferable to multi-camera parking lot coverage — [Multi-Camera Multi-Vehicle Tracking Guided by Highway Overlapping FoVs](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380471775_Multi-Camera_Multi-Vehicle_Tracking_Guided_by_Highway_Overlapping_FoVs)

**BEV / homography refinement**
- To address IPM's blurring/stretching artifacts for distant or tall objects, mitigation approaches include "a generative adversarial network or training a BEV decoder with synthetic ground-truth... to refine the IPM projection" — [Emergent Mind: Camera-to-BEV Projection Techniques](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/camera-to-bev-projection)
- Beyond IPM refinement, learned BEV semantic segmentation methods exist as alternatives to geometric projection entirely, e.g. Lift-Splat-Shoot, which "completes the view transformation from image view to BEV with pixel-wise depth estimation," and BEVSegFormer / LaRa / GitNet, which perform BEV semantic segmentation "from arbitrary camera rigs" using transformer-based cross-view attention rather than a fixed planar homography — [OpenDriveLab BEV Camera Paper List](https://github.com/OpenDriveLab/Birds-eye-view-Perception/blob/master/docs/paper_list/bev_camera.md); [BEVSegFormer, arXiv 2203.04050](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.04050); [LaRa, arXiv 2206.13294](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13294)
- These deep BEV methods were developed primarily for vehicle-mounted multi-camera autonomous-driving rigs, not fixed infrastructure cameras — direct transfer to a single fixed parking camera is not documented in the sources found (see Gaps).

**Temporal tracking**
- Standard practice: "During occlusion, when an object is temporarily obscured, the Kalman Filter can continue to predict the object's state based on its previous trajectory, and once the object reappears, the Hungarian algorithm helps to re-establish the correct association" — [search synthesis of Kalman-filter occlusion-handling literature](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6935235/)
- DeepSORT-style tracking "improves on SORT... using the Kalman filter algorithm for trajectory preprocessing and state estimation correlated with the Hungarian algorithm," adding appearance (re-ID) features "to realize tracking of an object occluded for a long time" — directly applicable to bridging multi-frame occlusion in parking video — [search synthesis](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/app/uploads/2024/03/MSR_thesis.pdf)
- A parking-lot-specific study (Paidi et al. 2021, Journal of Advanced Transportation) used a Kalman filter that "maintained data association during missed detection," keeping an assigned vehicle ID alive "for 50 frames" to survive occlusion/missed-detection gaps — a concrete, parking-domain-specific tracking parameter — [Tracking Vehicle Cruising in an Open Parking Lot Using Deep Learning and Kalman Filter](https://doi.org/10.1155/2021/1812647)
- A CMU Robotics Institute thesis found "a basic Kalman filter can obtain state-of-the-art tracking performance if proper care is taken to fix the noise accumulated during occlusion, using object observations to compute a virtual trajectory over the occlusion period" — i.e., careful noise handling matters more than filter sophistication — [Improving Kalman Filter-based MOT in Occlusion and Non-linear Motion, CMU RI thesis](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/app/uploads/2024/03/MSR_thesis.pdf)
- Unscented Kalman Filters (UKF) are used specifically "to estimate the motion state of multi-object [and] solve nonlinear errors" where vehicle motion (turning into a spot) violates the linear-motion assumption of a standard KF — [Achieving Adaptive Visual MOT with UKF](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9741288/)

**Segmentation vs. bounding boxes**
- "Instance segmentation only needs to find the edge contour of the object of interest with no need for bounding boxes, hence it can realize a more accurate object detection when assessing the behaviors of vehicles" — and segmentation masks let per-pixel area be measured directly, useful for "estimating the proportion of road space occupied by vehicles" — i.e., directly applicable to slot-polygon overlap/occupancy computation — [Voxel51: Image Segmentation Maps vs Bounding Boxes](https://voxel51.com/blog/why-are-image-segmentation-maps-superior-to-bounding-boxes)
- Mask R-CNN "returns the pixel-wise mask for each detected object," cited as the baseline algorithm choice "for the task of accurately quantifying parking space utilization" — [search synthesis of segmentation-for-parking literature](https://medium.com/geoai/parking-lot-vehicle-detection-using-deep-learning-49597917bc4a)
- The Holistic Parking Slot Network (HPS-Net) explicitly rejects bounding boxes in favor of four-point polygon (quadrilateral) representation for parking slots because "for diagonal slots or partially occluded slots, the visible corners form a four-point polygon of any shape" that a bounding box cannot represent; their polygon representation "naturally accommodates occlusions by precisely fitting the visible area in occlusion cases," using a custom "polygon-corner GIoU" loss that preserves corner ordering for orientation determination — [Holistic Parking Slot Detection with Polygon-Shaped Representations, arXiv 2310.11629](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11629v1)
- HPS-Net (trained on their own 13,964-image, 104,330-slot VPSD dataset covering a 25m×25m area) achieves F1 0.92 on normal parking, 0.91 on paving, 0.86 on indoor scenes on their internal dataset, and F1 0.99 on the public PS2.0 dataset — [arXiv 2310.11629](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11629v1)
- Oriented bounding boxes (OBB) are a middle ground between axis-aligned boxes and full polygon/segmentation: OBB "introduces an extra angle to locate objects more accurately," output as (x, y, w, h, r) in the Ultralytics YOLO-OBB format, and is "particularly useful when objects appear at various angles" as in aerial/angled parking imagery, avoiding the unnecessary background area that axis-aligned boxes include — [Ultralytics OBB Docs](https://docs.ultralytics.com/tasks/obb)
- An enhanced YOLOv5-OBB architecture for parking scenarios adds "an additional angle prediction branch and Coordinate Attention mechanisms" on top of an optimized backbone/PANet neck — a concrete architecture pattern for adapting a YOLO detector to output oriented vehicle footprints — [Enhanced YOLOv5-OBB for parking, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Enhanced-YOLOv5-OBB-model-architecture-for-oriented-bounding-box-detection-in-parking_fig4_393602743)
- YOLO-HG (built on YOLOv8) is reported to achieve "excellent performance on heavy-duty vehicle parking space datasets" using a "hierarchical global perception" design, though specific numeric results were not retrieved — [YOLO-HG, Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-95-2101-2_31)
- The Holistic Parking Slot Network (predecessor line of work) adapts YOLOv4 to directly output four vertex coordinates of parking slots in the top-view domain "instead of bounding boxes in raw camera images" — an end-to-end one-step alternative to detect-then-project pipelines — [search synthesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11629v1)

### Inferences
- The four mitigation layers are complementary and stackable: multi-camera overlap solves the geometric occlusion problem that no amount of per-frame CV cleverness can solve from a single viewpoint; temporal tracking solves *momentary* occlusion within a single camera's view; polygon/OBB/segmentation representations solve the *representation* problem (bounding boxes are a poor fit for angled/partially-visible vehicles) but do not by themselves solve true full occlusion (a vehicle 100% hidden behind another cannot be segmented from pixels that don't exist).
- For a system already using YOLO + homography + oriented footprint + polygon overlap (as the user's system does), the most directly applicable and evidence-backed upgrade path is: (a) adopt OBB or a polygon/quadrilateral output head instead of axis-aligned bboxes (concrete precedent: YOLOv5-OBB, HPS-Net, YOLO-HG), (b) add Kalman-filter-based tracklet smoothing with an explicit "hold ID alive for N frames" occlusion-survival window (concrete precedent: 50-frame window in Paidi et al.), and (c) if a second camera angle is feasible, prioritize physical overlap coverage over software-only fixes, since overlap is reported to reduce occlusion impact more directly than any single-camera algorithmic technique.

### Gaps
- No source found that describes a BEVFormer-style (multi-camera transformer cross-attention) approach specifically adapted to *fixed* infrastructure cameras (as opposed to vehicle-mounted rigs for autonomous driving) — this is a notable literature gap; BEV transformer methods (BEVFormer, BEVSegFormer, LaRa, GitNet) are documented for AV perception stacks, but no parking-lot-specific adaptation paper was found in this search pass.
- No quantitative before/after accuracy comparison was found isolating the specific benefit of "adding Kalman-filter tracking" to a parking occupancy pipeline (e.g., X% accuracy without tracking vs. Y% with tracking) — sources describe the technique and qualitative benefit but not a controlled ablation number in the parking-occupancy domain specifically.

## What benchmark datasets/papers (2023-2026) report accuracy numbers, and what techniques are state-of-the-art?

### Takeaway
PKLot and CNRPark-EXT remain the dominant classification-only benchmarks (used by 66.7% of recent studies) with saturated accuracy (~97-99%), but newer 2023-2025 datasets (ACPDS, CRPS-D) that test cross-lot generalization and realistic conditions (occlusion, slant, weather, damaged markings) show meaningfully lower and more representative accuracy, and are more relevant to a single-camera field deployment.

### Cited Findings
- PKLot: 12,416-12,417 images, ~695,900-700,000 manually labeled parking spaces across three lots (PUCPR, UFPR04, UFPR05), under sunny/cloudy/rainy weather — [PMC10490723](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10490723/); [PKLot on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Voxel51/PKLot)
- CNRPark-EXT: ~144,965-160,000 labeled parking-space image patches from 9 static camera viewpoints of a single lot, under sunny/overcast/rainy conditions and varying camera-to-spot distances — [PMC10490723](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10490723/)
- An improved MobileNetV3 model (2023-24) achieved 98.01% average accuracy on combined CNRPark-EXT + PKLot, vs. 97.03% for "CarNet"; AUC 0.99 for most PKLot experiments — [Yuldashev et al., Parking Lot Occupancy Detection with Improved MobileNetV3, Sensors 2023 (with 2024 correction)](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/17/7642); [PMC11360086 (correction)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11360086/)
- ACPDS (Almeida/de Almeida Coelho Parking Dataset — image-based occupancy classification benchmark): 293 images captured at ~10m height with a GoPro Hero 6, each image from a unique parking lot/view, train/val/test split by *unique lots* (i.e., testing generalization to unseen lots, not just unseen images of a seen lot) — a baseline model reaches 98% accuracy on unseen parking lots — [Image-Based Parking Space Occupancy Classification: Dataset and Baseline, arXiv 2107.12207](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.12207)
- CRPS-D (2025): 35,379 images (29,803 train / 5,936 test), 8 scene categories (indoor low/bright light, outdoor daylight/rainy/shadow/night, slanted slots, damaged markings), ~2.94x larger than ps2.0 and ~1.57x larger than SNU dataset, 11.75% slanted slots vs. 3.41% in ps2.0 — [arXiv 2509.13133](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13133v1)
- On CRPS-D, a semi-supervised method (SS-PSD, teacher-student EMA + confidence-guided mask consistency + Adaptive-VAT feature perturbation) using only 1/12 of labeled data reached 79.75% AP, beating the fully-labeled-data DMPR-PS baseline's 66.84% AP under the same low-label condition by 12.91 points — and on ps2.0 under the same 1/12-label condition, SS-PSD reached 90.47% AP vs. DMPR-PS's 78.05% — demonstrating semi-supervised learning as a state-of-the-art technique for label-scarce, realistic parking-slot detection — [arXiv 2509.13133](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13133v1)
- Under full supervision, DMPR-PS reaches 87.20% AP on CRPS-D vs. 98.64% AP on ps2.0 — confirming CRPS-D is a substantially harder, more realistic benchmark — [arXiv 2509.13133](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13133v1)
- HPS-Net (polygon-based) reaches F1 0.99 on the public PS2.0 dataset and F1 0.92/0.91/0.86 (normal/paving/indoor) on its own internal VPSD dataset (13,964 images, 104,330 slots, 25m×25m coverage) — [arXiv 2310.11629](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11629v1)
- A January 2025 systematic review, "Autonomous On-Street Parking Slot Detection: A Comprehensive Systematic Review of Vision-Based Approaches," categorizes deep-learning parking-slot-detection methods into object detection, image segmentation, regression, and graph neural network approaches, noting GNNs as a newer addition to the taxonomy — [search result title/abstract only; full text not fetched](https://etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/view/11317)
- YOLOv11s achieved mAP@0.5:0.95 of 0.8763 for parking occupancy detection under a strict train/test partition with different acquisition dates and camera viewpoints (testing real-world generalization rather than same-distribution accuracy) — [search synthesis, UAV/aerial vehicle detection literature](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-2688/7/2/74)

### Inferences
- There is a clear split in the literature between "classification-style" datasets (PKLot/CNRPark-EXT: is this pre-defined rectangle occupied? ~97-99% accuracy, largely saturated) and "detection-style" datasets (ps2.0, CRPS-D, VPSD: find and orient the slot/vehicle from scratch, 66-98% AP depending on difficulty). A single-camera YOLO + homography + polygon-overlap system (as in the user's stack) is architecturally closer to the harder detection-style problem, so PKLot/CNRPark 97-99% figures are not a realistic accuracy ceiling to benchmark against — CRPS-D-style numbers (79-87% AP under realistic conditions) are more representative.
- Cross-lot/unseen-lot generalization (as tested by ACPDS) is a distinct and often-overlooked failure axis from within-lot occlusion/lighting robustness — a model tuned on one physical lot's camera geometry may not transfer well to a new site without re-calibration, which is directly relevant to any multi-site commercial deployment.

## What role does camera placement/height/angle play in mitigating occlusion?

### Takeaway
Industry CCTV/ITS guidance and academic parking-dataset design converge on a moderate-elevation oblique mounting (roughly 4-12m height, 15-45° depression angle) as the practical sweet spot; going near-top-down (drone/high aerial, ~10m+ directly overhead) minimizes occlusion most effectively but is impractical for fixed infrastructure, while low, steep-angle mounting (as on a lamppost, ~12m or less) reproduces the same occlusion patterns seen in ground-level datasets.

### Cited Findings
- General CCTV/parking-lot guidance: mount cameras "on light poles or dedicated CCTV poles at 4 to 5 meters height, spaced every 25 to 35 meters," though broader security-camera guidance suggests 10-30 feet (~3-9m), "with taller poles needed to overcome any objects or structures that may block the line of sight" — [CCTV Camera Placement Guide 2026](https://cctvplanner.io/blog/camera-placement-guide); [Lightmart: Security Camera System Design for Parking Lots](https://www.lightmart.com/blog/how-to-design-a-security-camera-system-for-parking-lots-pole-placement-height-coverage/)
- For license-plate-capture-oriented placement, camera angle should be kept low, "many vendors recommend staying under about 30°" for plate legibility, while general surveillance angle guidance suggests "an effective camera angle is between 15° and 45°" — [Shunjie Barrier: LPR Camera Placement Guide](https://shunjiebarrier.com/blog/lpr-camera-placement-for-parking-angles-shutter-speed-ir-illumination-and-occlusion-control/)
- Explicitly on occlusion vs. height: "mounting height and pitch affect projected object size and therefore recall, which impacts occlusion visibility. A camera installed on a lamppost typically at most 12 meters above the ground would result in the same view angles and same levels of occlusions, whereas a dataset captured from high above the ground would not include significant occlusions" — this is a direct, quantified statement that low/moderate mounting (≤12m) does *not* solve occlusion, and only substantially higher (near-overhead) mounting does — [search synthesis, camera placement/occlusion literature](https://www.checkvideo.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/CheckVideo_Camera_Placement_Guide.pdf)
- ACPDS (academic parking occupancy dataset) was deliberately captured at ~10m height with a handheld GoPro to approximate a realistic elevated-but-not-aerial vantage point achievable on typical infrastructure — [arXiv 2107.12207](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.12207)
- Drone/aerial studies (DJI Mini 3, "central and oblique perspectives," 3-axis gimbal) show that even from an aerial platform, oblique (non-nadir) viewing angles still reduce detection confidence under shadows/occlusion, i.e., height alone does not eliminate the occlusion problem if the angle remains oblique — only a genuinely top-down/near-nadir view does — [DJI Mini 3 + YOLOv11 study, MDPI Drones 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-2688/7/2/74)
- General multi-camera design guidance: maintain "at least 10-20% overlap between camera coverage" to avoid blind spots — directly applicable as a placement rule when a single elevated camera cannot see all slots without occlusion — [CCTV Camera Placement Guide 2026](https://cctvplanner.io/blog/camera-placement-guide)

### Inferences
- There is an inherent height/practicality trade-off: the literature's own numbers show that occlusion is not meaningfully reduced until mounting height substantially exceeds typical lamppost/CCTV-pole height (>12m, approaching drone/rooftop altitude) — for most real installations (light poles, building facades), height alone will not solve occlusion, and oblique angle (even from a drone) still causes confidence drops in shadow/occlusion zones. This reinforces that the multi-camera-overlap and temporal-tracking mitigations (previous section) are not optional extras but necessary complements to any single-camera mounting strategy, however elevated.
- For angled/perpendicular parking rows specifically (called out in the assignment), the recommended mitigation is not mounting angle alone but positioning the camera so its viewing axis is as close to *perpendicular to the row axis* as the site allows (to minimize row-wise vehicle-on-vehicle self-occlusion), combined with the ≥10-20% multi-camera overlap rule for any single row that a single camera cannot fully de-occlude.
