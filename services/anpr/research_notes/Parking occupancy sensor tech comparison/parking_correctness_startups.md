## Are there specific startups/vendors marketing "parking correctness," "compliance," "space violation," or "improperly parked vehicle detection" as a distinct feature? Is "Parkobot" (the user's reference) actually one of these?

### Takeaway
Parkobot — the system explicitly named in the brief — is **not** a computer-vision parking-correctness product. It is a Kolkata-based IoT/hardware startup that makes physical smart parking barriers/locks for renting out private parking spaces by the hour (an "Airbnb for parking spaces" model), not an angle/alignment detection system. Genuine "parking correctness" as a distinct marketed feature is a small, thinly-documented niche: only two vendors were found explicitly claiming it — **Parquery** (Swiss, occupancy analytics, angled/across/protruding detection as a robustness claim) and **Parksol** (Lithuania-based, markets a dedicated "Parking Violation System" for "incorrectly parked vehicles"). ## How does Parkobot (or similar systems) specifically claim to detect "correct" vs "incorrect" parking — oriented bounding boxes vs. slot polygon, angle threshold, IoU with defined slot lines?

### Takeaway
Parkobot itself makes no such claim (see above — it is hardware/booking, not vision). However, the well-documented **method used across patents and research** for this exact problem is: (1) establish a reference/nominal orientation for the parking line or slot (via Hough transform on the scene, traffic heat-mapping, or manual calibration), (2) compute the detected vehicle's own orientation (via edge detection + Hough transform on the vehicle silhouette, or ellipse-fitting, or a rotated/oriented bounding box regression), and (3) compare the angular difference against a threshold — this is the core technique in the oldest and most detailed patent found (Xerox/Modaxo, filed 2012). Modern deep-learning variants replace steps 1–2 with a trained oriented-bounding-box (OBB) regression head instead of classical Hough/edge methods, but the final "compare angle (or IoU) to threshold" logic is conceptually the same.

### Cited Findings
- US Patent US20130266187A1 / US8737690B2, "Video-based method for parking angle violation detection" (Xerox Corp, now assigned to Modaxo Traffic Management USA Inc; inventors Orhan Bulan, Robert P. Loce, Edgar A. Bernal, Yao Rong Wang, Graham S. Pennington; filed May 1, 2012; published Oct 10, 2013) describes: nominal curb/parking-line angle (θc) determined via (a) Hough transform to find the longest line in an edge map of the static scene, (b) heat-mapping of traffic flow direction, or (c) manual calibration of curb coordinates; vehicle angle (θv) determined via edge detection + Hough transform on the vehicle's binary silhouette to find its longest axis, ellipse-regression fitting, or centroid analysis to find the orthogonal line pair with highest length ratio; violation is flagged when |θc − θv| exceeds a predetermined threshold T, which is set per relevant legislation and can vary by image location to correct for camera perspective distortion. — [Google Patents US20130266187A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20130266187A1/en)
- General computer-vision literature on oriented bounding boxes (OBB) for parking/vehicle detection confirms OBBs add an orientation parameter (often normalized −1 to +1) to standard (x, y, width, height) boxes via an added orientation-regression loss term during training, and that OBBs fit parking scenarios better than axis-aligned boxes specifically because vehicles in dense/angled arrangements have large aspect ratios that axis-aligned boxes fit poorly. — [PMC9658642 — Oriented Vehicle Detection in Aerial Images Based on YOLOv4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9658642/)
- An adapted Faster R-CNN oriented-bounding-box detector has been used in satellite-imagery parking-car detection pipelines, and general parking-space detection networks are described as predicting parking-space OBBs "through regression heads" — i.e., the slot itself (not just the vehicle) can be represented as an oriented box to compare against. — [MDPI: Detection of Parking Cars in Stereo Satellite Images](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/13/2170)
- The open-source YOLOv8 + OpenCV violation detector (non-commercial) uses a simpler geometric-overlap approach rather than angle comparison: it checks bounding-box intersection with the marked-stall polygon and flags a violation only when the vehicle sits "mostly outside" the stall into the driving lane, filtered by a 5-second stationarity timer to exclude vehicles briefly passing through. — [GitHub: sanjarbek1030/AutomaticParkingViolationDetector](https://github.com/sanjarbek1030/AutomaticParkingViolationDetector)
- In automated valet parking (AVP) research, parking-slot "confidence scores" are computed from geometric properties of detected slot-line pairs: slot width falling within a predefined range, verification that the two long slot-boundary lines are parallel, and a "supplementary angle" property; a width-based confidence sub-score is computed as Cw = 1 − (|WidthReal − WidthAssumed| / WidthReal). Separately, AVP "comfort scoring" for the vehicle's own final resting alignment considers margin to open doors on both sides and the straightness/variance of the vehicle's trajectory offset. — [PMC13259402 — Hierarchical NMPC/TD3 Framework for Automated Valet Parking](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13259402/)
- Chinese patent CN110688902A ("Method and device for detecting vehicle area in parking space") describes detecting the vehicle region within a parking space via image rotation at multiple angles combined with a vehicle-detection model — an alternate, more brute-force approach to angle handling (search over rotations) rather than direct angle regression. — [Google Patents CN110688902A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN110688902A/en)
- Chinese patent CN104157143A ("Illegal parking detection system and detection method thereof") was identified in search results as a dedicated illegal-parking detection patent, though full method detail was not fetched/verified in this research pass. — [Google Patents CN104157143A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN104157143A/en)

### Inferences
- The Xerox/Modaxo 2012 patent is likely the most detailed and citable canonical method for "angle-threshold" parking-violation detection publicly available — it predates the deep-learning era and uses classical computer vision (Hough transforms, edge detection, ellipse fitting), which suggests that even in 2025-2026, commercial "angle violation" products may still fundamentally rely on this same reference-angle-vs-vehicle-angle-threshold logic, just with a CNN/OBB regressor replacing the Hough-transform step for vehicle-angle estimation.
- No source found describes a standardized IoU-based "correctness score" (i.e., treating the slot as a polygon and measuring vehicle-polygon overlap as a percentage) as a named industry-standard metric for parking correctness — the two dominant paradigms found are (a) angle-threshold comparison (patents, violation-specific systems) and (b) simple bounding-box-vs-stall-polygon overlap/protrusion check (the open-source project), suggesting the field has not converged on one standard method.

### Gaps
- No confirmed technical detail on Parquery's or Parksol's actual underlying algorithm (OBB, IoU, or angle threshold) — both companies keep this proprietary/undisclosed in public marketing materials.
- Could not access full text of IEEE paper "Parking Violation Detection using Computer Vision for Urban Traffic Management" (ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11076537) — the fetch returned empty content, likely paywalled; a ResearchGate mirror exists (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394753789_Parking_Violation_Detection_using_Computer_Vision_for_Urban_Traffic_Management) but was not independently verified in this pass.
- Could not verify whether any 2024-2026 vendor explicitly uses "oriented bounding box" terminology in customer-facing marketing (as opposed to only in patents/academic papers) — this term does not appear to have crossed into product marketing language yet.

## Are there products aimed at vehicles spanning two spaces, parking diagonally/skewed, blocking fire lanes, or parking outside marked lines, for enforcement or space-optimization purposes?

### Takeaway
Yes, but the market splits cleanly into two unrelated groups: (1) general-purpose smart-city/campus traffic-enforcement cameras that detect *zone-based* violations (restricted lanes, bus lanes, fire lanes, no-parking zones, time-limited stops) via presence/duration logic rather than in-bay geometry, and (2) a much smaller set of parking-lot occupancy vendors (Parquery, the open-source YOLOv8 project, Parksol) that claim to specifically flag geometric misalignment (angled, across-boundary, protruding, or straddling-two-spaces) within a marked lot. No vendor was found marketing "fire lane blocking" as a distinct product separate from general restricted-zone/no-parking-zone enforcement.

### Cited Findings
- Multi-violation edge-AI cameras (e.g., referenced in traffic-enforcement coverage) can detect "up to 12 different traffic violations," described as including "stop violations, U-turns, emergency lane violations, wrong way driving" via "different line and area-based detectors" — emergency/fire-lane enforcement is handled as one of many zone-based violation types, not a dedicated product. — [Traffic Technology Today](https://www.traffictechnologytoday.com/features/feature-ai-takes-traffic-enforcement-beyond-anpr.html) (as characterized in search synthesis; original article not independently fetched)
- Chicago's "Smart Streets" pilot (camera-based enforcement of bus/bike lane and loading-zone violations) began issuing violations in December 2024 and was set to run through December 2026, later expanding to unpaid-meter and commercial-loading-zone violations in early 2026 — an example of zone/duration-based (not in-bay-geometry-based) automated enforcement at city scale. — [Chicago CDOT press release, Oct 2025](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/cdot/provdrs/transit_facilities/news/2025/october/city-of-chicago-and-cta-expand-smart-streets-pilot-with-automate.html), [Streetsblog Chicago, Jan 2026](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2026/01/28/stay-in-your-lane-or-pay-the-fine)
- Philadelphia Parking Authority's "Smart Loading Zones" pilot (since May 2025) issued 122,811 tickets (~8,000/month average) via cameras that report a vehicle after it parks longer than ~3 minutes in a zone without registering in the CurbPass system — again duration/registration-based, not geometric alignment. — [Billy Penn, Aug 2026](https://billypenn.com/2026/08/05/smart-loading-zones-philadelphia-parking-center-city-tickets/)
- Parquery explicitly claims detection robustness (not necessarily flagged-as-violation output) for vehicles "parked improperly, at an angle, across, or protrude beyond the boundaries" — i.e., cars spanning two spaces or crossing lines are within its stated detection envelope, framed as ensuring accurate occupancy counts despite messy real-world parking rather than as a compliance-ticketing feature. — [Parquery](https://parquery.com/parquery-and-ai/)
- The open-source YOLOv8/OpenCV project is the only source found with an explicit, code-level description of a "crossing into the driving lane" violation check using bounding-box geometry against a defined stall boundary, filtered by a stationarity timer — illustrative of feasibility rather than a commercial product. — [GitHub: sanjarbek1030/AutomaticParkingViolationDetector](https://github.com/sanjarbek1030/AutomaticParkingViolationDetector)

### Inferences
- Fire-lane and emergency-lane enforcement, where it exists commercially, appears to be handled by the same zone/area-detector infrastructure used for bus lanes and no-parking zones (a polygon "restricted area" + dwell-time trigger) rather than by a specialized fire-lane-specific product or vendor — the enforcement logic doesn't need to distinguish *why* a lane is restricted, only that a vehicle is present/stopped within a defined polygon for too long.
- "Space-optimization" framing (distinct from ticketing/enforcement) — i.e., detecting misparked cars specifically to reclaim lost capacity in owner/operator-run lots — appears to be the more common commercial framing (Parquery's angle is about accurate occupancy counting, not fines) versus "enforcement" framing (Parksol, city pilots) which is about issuing citations. This matches the two different buyer motivations: parking-lot operators care about not undercounting available spaces because of a badly parked car, while cities/enforcement agencies care about ticketing.

### Gaps
- No vendor or product was found that explicitly names "fire lane blocking by a parked vehicle" (as opposed to moving-traffic emergency-lane violations, which is a different and much better-documented use case) as a marketed feature.
- No confirmed source quantifying how common "straddling two spaces" detection is as a deployed (vs. claimed) feature — Parquery's claim is from their own marketing copy and was not independently verified via a case study, review, or third party.

## Adjacent markets — automated valet parking (AVP) / autonomous parking assist systems incorporating slot-alignment/correctness scoring

### Takeaway
AVP and autonomous-parking-assist research explicitly incorporates alignment/orientation scoring as a core, well-established part of the *vehicle's own* parking maneuver evaluation (not third-party monitoring) — this is a mature, if narrow, sub-field with concrete scoring formulas, and is the adjacent market most directly transferable to a "parking correctness" feature, since it already treats orientation and alignment as first-class, quantified metrics.

### Cited Findings
- In AVP path-planning/reinforcement-learning research, "correct heading alignment is essential for successful docking into a narrow parking slot," and orientation error is given "a relatively strong exponential penalty" in the reward-shaping function used to train the parking-maneuver policy — i.e., alignment is directly optimized against, not just measured after the fact. — [PMC13259402 — Hierarchical NMPC and TD3-Based Framework for AVP](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13259402/)
- The same AVP framework uses a "multidimensional evaluation system" with completion rate as the primary index and "operation performance" (parking efficiency and accuracy) as a secondary index — one described algorithm achieved a "final score of 94.2 points" under this methodology, indicating a quantified, published scoring rubric for parking-maneuver quality exists in this literature. — [PMC13259402](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13259402/)
- Parking-slot "confidence scores" in AVP slot-detection research are computed from geometric properties of the detected slot itself: width within a predefined valid range, parallelism of the two long boundary lines, and a "supplementary angle" property, combined into a formula such as Cw = 1 − (|WidthReal − WidthAssumed| / WidthReal) for the width sub-score — this is a directly transferable technique for scoring how well a detected vehicle position matches an expected/valid slot geometry. — [PMC13259402](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13259402/)
- "Comfort scoring" in the same AVP research area separately evaluates the vehicle's final resting alignment using margin-to-open-doors on both sides and the straightness/variance of the vehicle's trajectory offset — a proxy metric that a stationary-monitoring "correctness" system could adapt (e.g., measuring symmetric clearance to both neighboring slot lines) even without access to the vehicle's own trajectory data. — [PMC13259402](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13259402/)

### Inferences
- AVP research solves a superficially different problem (guiding a vehicle *into* correct alignment in real time) but has already built and published the exact scoring primitives (angle penalty, slot-width/parallelism confidence, door-clearance symmetry) that a third-party overhead-camera "parking correctness" detector would need to adapt for a static, after-the-fact judgment — this is a stronger and more transferable technical foundation than anything found in the dedicated "parking violation" vendor space, where methods are kept proprietary/undisclosed.

### Gaps
- No source found directly repurposing AVP alignment-scoring formulas for third-party/overhead monitoring (all AVP sources describe onboard/ego-vehicle systems, not external camera-based correctness checking) — this repurposing appears to be a genuine unaddressed opportunity rather than something already attempted and documented, based on this search.
- Did not identify specific commercial AVP vendors (e.g., Bosch, Valeo, ZF systems) or their public technical marketing on this exact scoring point — the source found (PMC13259402) is academic/research rather than a named commercial AVP product, so it is unclear whether any commercial AVP system exposes or markets an alignment "score" externally.
