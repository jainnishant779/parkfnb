# Camera-Based and Novel Overhead Parking Occupancy Sensors (2025-2026)

## What are the leading single/multi-camera computer-vision parking occupancy vendors and how does each system work?

### Takeaway
The market is dominated by overhead/elevated camera systems that each cover a small cluster of spaces (typically 1-6, mounted per drive-aisle bay) for indoor garages, and wide-area cameras that cover large batches of spaces (50-150) for open lots/on-street use. Several named vendors from the assignment brief (Parkobot, Sensity, AeyeVironment, Robotic Research, Aeye) could **not** be independently verified as active parking-occupancy vendors via search — see Gaps. The verifiable, currently active players split into two architecture families: (1) per-bay indoor "smart sensor" cameras (Park Assist/TKH, INDECT/Upsolut, Circontrol, Milesight indoor) and (2) wide-area/outdoor multi-space cameras (Cleverciti, Parklio Detect, Parkinto, ParKam, Parquery, Urbiotica U-Flow VISIO, Intercomp, Milesight outdoor).

### Cited Findings
- **Park Assist (TKH Security)** — M5 Smart-Sensor: one or two HD CMOS digital cameras per unit, ceiling-mounted above the drive aisle, each sensor monitors up to 6 spaces (up to 3 on each side of the lane); built-in full-spectrum RGB LED for space-status indication; unit also does LPR and streams/stores HD video to the cloud (INX™ platform) for up to 90 days; IP66-rated housing. — [TKH Security M5 Smart-Sensor](https://tkhsecurity.com/park-assist-parking-guidance-system-m5-smart-sensor/)
- **Cleverciti** (acquired by ParkHelp, announced November 2025) — "Cleverciti Sensor" uses high-resolution dual-4K optical sensors, edge computing + AI, and can cover up to 100 parking spaces from a single sensor unit; measures the exact position and size of open spaces (not just occupied/vacant per stall) for on-street and off-street guidance and curb management. — [Cleverciti Sensor](https://www.cleverciti.com/en/innovations/cleverciti-sensor); [ParkHelp Acquires Cleverciti (BusinessWire, Nov 2025)](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251103685043/en/ParkHelp-Acquires-Cleverciti-Creating-the-Worlds-Most-Advanced-Platform-for-Parking-Guidance-and-Curb-Management)
- **INDECT** — merged globally with Parking Guidance Systems and ParkZen (announced September 2025); product line includes both single-space ultrasonic sensors and "Upsolut" camera sensors that combine multi-space monitoring, video stream capture, and license plate recognition. — [Parking Guidance Systems Announces Global Merger With INDECT and ParkZen (BusinessWire, Sept 2025)](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250908581978/en/Parking-Guidance-Systems-Announces-Global-Merger-With-INDECT-and-ParkZen); [INDECT Single Space Ultrasonic Sensors](https://indect.com/single-space-ultrasonic-sensors/)
- **Parking Logix** — flagship product "OpenSpace" is actually a wireless/embedded-sensor (speed-hump-embedded) counting solution paired with variable message signs and analytics software, not a camera system, despite the vendor's name; positioned as a low-cost counting solution rather than per-stall guidance. — [Parking Logix](https://parkinglogix.com/)
- **Parklio Detect** — overhead camera, up to ~70 outdoor spaces per camera, ~99% detection accuracy claimed. — [Parklio Top 10 Camera-Based Parking Occupancy Systems review](https://parklio.com/en/blog/top-10-camera-based-parking-occupancy-detection-systems-2024-review-guide)
- **Parkinto** — claims 99.7% free/occupied detection accuracy, up to 100 spaces monitored per camera. — [search result summary citing Parkinto](https://parkinto.com/)
- **ParKam** — up to 150 outdoor spaces per camera, ~99% accuracy claimed. — [Parklio Top 10 review](https://parklio.com/en/blog/top-10-camera-based-parking-occupancy-detection-systems-2024-review-guide)
- **Parquery** — positions itself as adding AI to existing/any camera ("upgrade any camera with artificial intelligence"); claims 95% accuracy "out-of-the-box," improving to 99% through on-site learning/calibration. — [Parquery](https://parquery.com/); [Parklio Top 10 review](https://parklio.com/en/blog/top-10-camera-based-parking-occupancy-detection-systems-2024-review-guide)
- **Urbiotica U-Flow VISIO** — up to 50 spaces per camera, ~99% reliability claimed. — [Parklio Top 10 review](https://parklio.com/en/blog/top-10-camera-based-parking-occupancy-detection-systems-2024-review-guide)
- **Smart Parking Systems by Intercomp** — ~99% accuracy claimed, capacity per camera varies by camera type/deployment. — [Parklio Top 10 review](https://parklio.com/en/blog/top-10-camera-based-parking-occupancy-detection-systems-2024-review-guide)
- **Circontrol** — indoor per-bay camera sensor, up to 6 spaces simultaneously per unit (same class as Park Assist/Milesight indoor); accuracy not disclosed in the source reviewed. — [Parklio Top 10 review](https://parklio.com/en/blog/top-10-camera-based-parking-occupancy-detection-systems-2024-review-guide)
- **Milesight** — two product classes: (1) indoor per-bay "Indoor Parking Guidance Camera" covering up to 6 spaces at ≥99.9% claimed accuracy, and (2) outdoor wide-area "AI Outdoor Parking Management Pro Bullet Plus Camera" covering up to 100 spaces at >98% claimed accuracy; outdoor unit is IP67 weatherproof and IK10 vandal-resistant with a heater module to clear lens fog. — [Milesight Indoor Parking Guidance Camera](https://www.milesight.com/security/product/indoor-parking-guidance-camera); [Milesight AI Outdoor Parking Management Camera](https://www.milesight.com/security/product/ai-parking-management-pro-bullet-plus-camera)
- **Amano McGann** — primarily an access-control/LPR and revenue-management vendor; deploys LPR cameras alongside existing in-ground sensors rather than offering a distinct proprietary per-stall occupancy-camera product line that could be verified in search results. — [Amano McGann on Parking Network](https://www.parking.net/parking-industry/amano-mcgann-inc)
- General architecture pattern confirmed across multiple vendors: **one overhead/elevated camera can replace roughly 70 in-ground sensors** in outdoor deployments (vendor-cited figure, exact source vendor unclear/aggregated). — [AirGarage: What Is a Camera-Based Parking System?](https://www.airgarage.com/resources/what-is-a-camera-based-parking-system-pros-costs-roi)

### Inferences
- The parking-guidance industry is actively consolidating: two major mergers were announced within weeks of each other in 2025 (ParkHelp/Cleverciti in November, and Parking Guidance Systems/INDECT/ParkZen in September), suggesting significant M&A activity and possible platform standardization around camera-based guidance in 2025-2026.
- There is a clear architectural split by venue type: **indoor garages** favor per-bay ceiling cameras covering 1-6 spaces (Park Assist M5, Circontrol, Milesight indoor, INDECT/Upsolut) because garage geometry (columns, low ceilings, drive aisles) limits sightlines; **outdoor lots and on-street parking** favor wide-area cameras covering 50-150 spaces (Cleverciti, Parkinto, ParKam, Urbiotica) because open sightlines allow one elevated camera to see many stalls.
- Vendors named in the assignment brief that could not be found (Parkobot, Sensity Systems, AeyeVironment/Aeye, Robotic Research as a parking-camera vendor) are either not real/active parking-occupancy brands, are very obscure/regional, or the names may be misremembered/conflated with similar-sounding companies (e.g., "Sensity" may be confused with Nedap's "SENSIT" ultrasonic sensor line, which is NOT a camera system).

### Gaps

These appear to be different products; could not confirm whether "Sensity Systems" (historically a smart-street-lighting/sensor-node company, per prior knowledge) is still active in parking specifically as of 2025-2026.
- **AeyeVironment / Aeye**: no parking-specific camera vendor by this name was found (AeroVironment is a drone/defense company; unclear if it has a parking product).
- **Robotic Research**: found only in the context of general autonomous-vehicle/parking-robot patents, not as a named commercial overhead-camera parking-occupancy vendor.

## What are their published/claimed accuracy rates, cost per space, and installation complexity compared to ground sensors?

### Takeaway
Camera vendors consistently claim 98-99.9% accuracy, and because one camera covers many spaces, effective cost-per-space is claimed to be lower than ground sensors at scale — but few vendors publish transparent, itemized cost-per-space figures; most cost data available is from third-party aggregator/buyer-guide estimates rather than vendor price sheets.

### Cited Findings
- **Accuracy claims by vendor** (all vendor-published, none independently audited in sources found):
  - Park Assist M5: 99%+ — [TKH Security](https://tkhsecurity.com/park-assist-parking-guidance-system-m5-smart-sensor/)
  - Cleverciti: no specific percentage found in public marketing copy; claims to be "the industry's most accurate parking sensor" per "multiple independent studies," but the studies themselves were not located. — [Cleverciti](https://www.cleverciti.com/en/why-cleverciti/what-is-smart-parking)
  - Milesight indoor: ≥99.9%; Milesight outdoor: >98% — [Milesight Indoor](https://www.milesight.com/security/product/indoor-parking-guidance-camera); [Milesight Outdoor](https://www.milesight.com/security/product/ai-parking-management-pro-bullet-plus-camera)
  - Parklio Detect: ~99%; Parkinto: 99.7%; ParKam: ~99%; Urbiotica U-Flow VISIO: ~99%; Intercomp: ~99% — [Parklio Top 10 review](https://parklio.com/en/blog/top-10-camera-based-parking-occupancy-detection-systems-2024-review-guide)
  - Parquery: 95% out-of-the-box, rising to 99% after on-site model tuning/learning — [Parquery](https://parquery.com/); [Parklio Top 10 review](https://parklio.com/en/blog/top-10-camera-based-parking-occupancy-detection-systems-2024-review-guide)
  - General academic/industry claim: some AI-powered camera solutions claim over 99.9% accuracy for cars, trucks, and motorcycles collectively. — [search synthesis, exact vendor unattributed](https://parklio.com/en/blog/parking-space-detection-sensors-vs-camera-comparison-review)
- **Cost figures (ground/legacy sensor baselines, for comparison):**
  - Ultrasonic parking sensors with LED indicators: $300-$500 per space installed. — [ITS Deployment Evaluation, US DOT](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2020-sc00464)
  - San Francisco ground-sensor deployment: ~$1,400 average total cost per sensor (device + admin + labor + logistics). — [Parklio blog synthesis](https://parklio.com/en/blog/parking-space-detection-sensors-vs-camera-comparison-review)
  - In-ground magnetic sensors: $50-$100/unit; overhead ultrasonic: $100-$200/unit; radar: $80-$150/unit; blended installed total: $100-$350 per space. — [Accio: Parking Lot Sensor System Cost 2026](https://www.accio.com/business/parking-lot-sensor-system-cost)
  - Ultrasonic sensors alone (device only, excluding gateway/labor): $50-$150 per spot. — [Accio: Parking Lot Sensor System Cost 2026](https://www.accio.com/business/parking-lot-sensor-system-cost)
- **Cost figures (camera-based systems):**
  - Camera-based solutions covering 10-20 spaces per camera: $200-$500 total installed cost for that cluster (implying roughly $10-$50 per space when amortized, though this is an inference — see Inferences). — [Accio: Parking Lot Sensor System Cost 2026](https://www.accio.com/business/parking-lot-sensor-system-cost)
  - LPR-specific camera installations: $330-$600 per space; a single ALPR camera unit costs $3,000-$8,000 installed. — [VDS: Parking Lot Security Camera Cost 2026](https://visiondetectionsystems.com/blog/parking-lot-security-camera-cost)
  - Standard commercial IP cameras (2-4MP), not parking-specific: $700-$900 installed per camera. — [VDS: Parking Lot Security Camera Cost 2026](https://visiondetectionsystems.com/blog/parking-lot-security-camera-cost)
  - Aggregate small-lot deployment (under 50 spaces): $5,000-$15,000 installed; medium lot (50-200 spaces): $15,000-$40,000 installed. — [VDS: Parking Lot Security Camera Cost 2026](https://visiondetectionsystems.com/blog/parking-lot-security-camera-cost)
- **Installation complexity:** Camera systems are generally described by vendors/aggregators as easier to install and maintain than dense ground-sensor grids because one unit serves many spaces, reducing the number of physical mounting/wiring points and pavement-cutting work (relevant for in-ground sensors specifically). — [AirGarage: What Is a Camera-Based Parking System?](https://www.airgarage.com/resources/what-is-a-camera-based-parking-system-pros-costs-roi)

### Inferences
- Vendor-published accuracy figures cluster tightly around 98-99.9% across nearly every company surveyed, which is suspicious from an epistemic standpoint — this narrow, uniformly high range suggests either (a) genuine technology convergence/maturity in the category, or (b) marketing-driven rounding/self-reporting without independent, standardized audit methodology. No third-party/independent benchmark comparing vendors head-to-head was found in this research pass.
- Because ground sensors are priced and typically reported per-device (which corresponds 1:1 with per-space), while camera systems are priced per-camera-covering-many-spaces, true apples-to-apples cost-per-space for cameras requires dividing a camera's installed cost by its space count — vendors rarely publish this division directly, forcing buyers (and this report) to infer it. The one aggregator figure found ($200-500 installed covering 10-20 spaces, i.e., ~$10-50/space) is dramatically lower than the equivalent ground-sensor figures ($100-1,400/space), which is directionally consistent with the industry's constant messaging that cameras are cheaper at scale, but this single data point should be treated as illustrative rather than authoritative.
- No vendor in this research disclosed subscription/software licensing costs (recurring SaaS fees per space per month), which are commonly part of camera-based smart-parking offerings (data platforms, cloud video storage, analytics dashboards) — this is a notable gap in available public pricing transparency across the entire category.

### Gaps
- No independent, third-party accuracy audit or head-to-head comparative benchmark of these vendors was found; all accuracy numbers are vendor-self-reported.
- No vendor published a clean, itemized "$X hardware + $Y installation + $Z/month software" cost breakdown per space; all cost figures found are either ground-sensor baselines or camera aggregator estimates, not vendor list prices.
- Recurring/subscription software costs for camera-based systems (a major cost component per most owner/operator discussions of vendor lock-in) were not disclosed in any source found.

## Are there stereo camera or depth-camera (RGB-D, ToF) based parking occupancy systems in commercial deployment in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
Yes — depth-based 3D vision is an emerging but real commercial category distinct from mainstream 2D camera vendors, positioned explicitly as solving 2D cameras' occlusion/lighting/shadow weaknesses; LIPS Corporation is a concrete example marketing a 3D depth-camera parking occupancy product as of 2025-2026, and general-purpose stereo/depth camera hardware (Intel RealSense D455, Stereolabs ZED 2) is used in parking-related research and pilot deployments, though evidence of large-scale commercial rollout (vs. pilot/research stage) is limited in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- **LIPS Corporation** markets a 3D-vision parking occupancy approach explicitly contrasted against 2D cameras: "3D cameras installed on-site capture RGB + depth data," processed by their "Measure Master SDK" using rule-based 3D measurement rather than visual/appearance classification; defines each parking space as a 3D zone and determines occupancy by measuring "height and volume changes within each zone" against a depth threshold sustained over time. Marketing claims this eliminates false positives from shadows and works reliably "in rain, strong sunlight, or low light" because depth measurement is not affected by color/texture/contrast variation the way 2D appearance-based classifiers are. The specific depth-sensing modality (ToF vs. structured light vs. stereo) was not disclosed in the page content retrieved, and no accuracy percentage or cost was published. — [LIPS: The 3D Revolution in Parking](https://www.lips-hci.com/post/the-3d-revolution-in-parking-why-precision-occupancy-is-a-measurement-problem-not-just-a-vision-task)
- General stereo-camera hardware relevant to parking/obstacle research: the **Stereolabs ZED 2** (120° field of view, 0.2-20m depth range) and **Intel RealSense D455** (on-chip self-calibration in under 15 seconds) are both cited in recent technical/research literature covering parking-lot-adjacent obstacle and occupancy detection, indicating these commodity depth-camera platforms are being used in research/pilot contexts. — [arXiv: Depth and Image Fusion for Road Obstacle Detection Using Stereo Camera (Jan 2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.07245)
- Academic framing supports the depth-camera value proposition: 2D cameras "measure appearance rather than physical state, relying heavily on color, texture, and contrast which break under environmental variability," motivating the shift toward 3D/depth-based occupancy classification research. — [LIPS: The 3D Revolution in Parking](https://www.lips-hci.com/post/the-3d-revolution-in-parking-why-precision-occupancy-is-a-measurement-problem-not-just-a-vision-task)

### Inferences
- Depth/3D-camera parking occupancy appears to be a real, distinct, and currently-marketed value proposition (not vaporware), explicitly targeting the known weak points of 2D camera systems: shadows, lighting changes, and occlusion. However, the fact that only one vendor (LIPS) surfaced clearly in searches, without disclosed accuracy numbers, without disclosed pricing, and without independent case studies, suggests this is an early-stage/niche category relative to the mature 2D-camera vendor ecosystem (Cleverciti, Park Assist, Milesight, etc.) which has multiple vendors with public accuracy claims and years of deployment history.
- The value proposition of stereo/depth over mono-2D is about **robustness to appearance-based failure modes** (shadows mistaken for cars, glare, low light, similar-colored pavement/vehicle) rather than necessarily higher raw accuracy in ideal conditions — this is an inference drawn from LIPS's own framing, not an independently verified benchmark comparison.

### Gaps
- No independent case study, accuracy percentage, or named large-scale commercial deployment site was found for any stereo/depth-camera parking occupancy vendor (LIPS or others) — this space appears to be represented in early-adopter marketing content rather than mature deployment data as of this research pass.
- Could not determine whether LIPS's product uses ToF, structured light, or stereo triangulation specifically — the term "3D camera" was used without technical disambiguation in the source retrieved.
- No pricing information was found for any depth/stereo-based commercial parking occupancy product, preventing any cost comparison against 2D camera systems.
- Other possible depth-camera parking vendors were not identified in this research pass beyond LIPS; the category may be broader than what surfaced in available search results (search engine coverage of small/newer vendors in this niche may be limited).

## Is there any real, recently launched (2024-2026) "3D hologram" type or volumetric/holographic overhead parking sensor product?

### Takeaway
**No genuine "hologram" or holographic-imaging parking occupancy sensor product was found.** The search for this specific term returned only unrelated technologies that happen to share vocabulary: (1) actual holographic image sensors and volumetric mid-air display technology exist as general-purpose optical/display technologies but are not applied to parking occupancy detection in any source found; (2) "holographic parking permits" is a real, established but entirely different product category — physical anti-counterfeit permit tags/decals with a hologram security feature, unrelated to occupancy sensing. The "3D"/volumetric language that does legitimately apply to parking occupancy in 2025-2026 refers to genuine depth-camera (RGB-D/ToF/stereo) technology (see previous section), which vendors like LIPS market using "3D," "depth," and "volume" language, but never the words "hologram" or "holographic."

### Cited Findings
- A general search for `"hologram parking sensor" OR "3D holographic occupancy sensor"` returned no matching commercial product; results instead surfaced (a) parking sensor patents using conventional technologies (magnetometer, LiDAR, piezo-electric, depth cameras), and (b) an unrelated Wikipedia article on "Holographic sensor" (a general chemical/optical sensing technology, not parking-specific), and (c) patents about in-vehicle holographic diagnostic displays (unrelated to occupancy detection). — [Google/Wikipedia/USPTO search results synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holographic_sensor)
- A follow-up search for `"volumetric parking detection" OR "holographic" parking sensor patent 2025` surfaced: a 2025 patent for general-purpose "volumetric display systems" (mid-air holographic image generation via laminar airflow) with no parking application; a 2025 "holographic image sensor" patent describing a general optical sensing apparatus (lens + wavelength filter + reference light source) with no parking application; and separate, unrelated 2025 parking sensor patents (active/odometry-based detection) that do not use holographic or volumetric terminology at all. — [Google Patents search synthesis](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20250020942A1/en); [Justia: Holographic image sensor patent](https://patents.justia.com/patent/12276939)
- A search for `"hologram" parking spot indicator garage marketing product 2025 2026` confirmed that "holographic" in parking-industry marketing refers exclusively to **holographic parking permits** — physical anti-counterfeit decals/tags for permit validation, a completely different product category from occupancy sensing, using holography only as a security/anti-duplication feature on paper/plastic permits. — [MyParkingPermit: Holographic Parking Permits](https://www.myparkingpermit.com/parking-permits/holographic-parking-pass)

### Inferences
- Based on the searches conducted, "3D hologram parking sensor" or "holographic occupancy sensor" is **not a real, distinct commercial product category** as of September 2026. It appears to be either (a) a misunderstanding/conflation of legitimate 3D depth-camera occupancy technology (RGB-D, ToF, stereo — real and commercially marketed, see LIPS above) with the unrelated, much more visually evocative word "hologram," or (b) a conflation with the entirely separate and well-established "holographic parking permit" security-tag product line, or (c) marketing terminology that may exist in a vendor's sales collateral or trade-show pitch but has not been indexed in any searchable trade press, patent database, or vendor website found in this research pass.
- If a vendor or trade-show source does describe a product as a "hologram parking sensor," the underlying technology in every case examined in this research turns out to be conventional depth-sensing (LiDAR, ToF, structured light, or stereo camera triangulation) rebranded with more dramatic marketing language — "hologram" and "holographic" were never found attached to a real, physically-holographic (interference-pattern-based 3D imaging) sensing product for parking anywhere in this research.

### Gaps
- This finding is based on English-language web search and patent database coverage; a genuinely obscure/early-stage startup product (e.g., unlaunched, trade-show-only, or non-English-market) using "hologram" marketing language could exist without being indexed yet. The research could not rule out a very recent (2026) or non-US/non-English product launch using this term.
- No trade press articles (Traffic Technology Today, Smart Cities World, ParkingNetwork, IPI publications) were directly searched/fetched for this specific term within the time available; a dedicated pass through those specific outlets' archives might surface a announcement not caught by general web search. This is flagged as an area for the report-writer or a follow-up researcher to double check if the "hologram" claim is considered high-priority to fully rule out.

## What do camera-vendor marketing materials/case studies claim about accuracy in occlusion-heavy or bad-weather conditions?

### Takeaway
Vendors uniformly claim strong all-weather performance through IP-rated weatherproof housings (IP66/IP67) plus AI/edge-computing image processing, but specific occlusion-scenario accuracy breakdowns (e.g., accuracy drop during heavy occlusion or snow cover) were not found in any vendor's public marketing copy — claims are consistently generic ("works in nearly any weather condition") rather than quantified per-condition.

### Cited Findings
- **Cleverciti** claims its sensors "can accurately detect parking availability in nearly any environmental or weather condition, be it heavy rain and snow or extreme heat and cold," attributing this to "patented technology" and "AI and edge-computing sensors [that] provide consistent reliability," and claims to be validated as "the industry's most accurate parking sensor" through unspecified "multiple independent studies." No quantified accuracy-by-weather-condition breakdown or citation to the underlying studies was found. — [Cleverciti: What is smart parking?](https://www.cleverciti.com/en/why-cleverciti/what-is-smart-parking)
- **Park Assist M5 (TKH Security)** relies on an IP66-rated housing to prevent water/dirt ingress as its primary stated weather-resilience mechanism; the 99%+ accuracy figure is presented as a general claim, not broken out specifically for adverse-weather or occlusion scenarios. — [TKH Security M5 Smart-Sensor](https://tkhsecurity.com/park-assist-parking-guidance-system-m5-smart-sensor/)
- **Milesight** outdoor camera uses IP67 weatherproofing, IK10 vandal-resistance, a dust-proof dome cover, weather-sealed connectors, and a **heater module specifically to clear lens fog/condensation**, marketed as enabling "24/7 parking management surveillance ... in rain, dust, and extreme temperatures" — this is the most specific hardware-level adverse-weather mitigation detail found among all vendors researched (the heater-for-fog detail is notable and concrete). No quantified accuracy-in-rain/snow percentage was found, however. — [Milesight AI Outdoor Parking Management Camera](https://www.milesight.com/security/product/ai-parking-management-pro-bullet-plus-camera); [Milesight blog: A Comprehensive Look at Parking Lot Cameras](https://www.milesight.com/company/blog/parking-lot-cameras)
- **LIPS Corporation** (depth-camera vendor) makes the occlusion/weather argument from the opposite direction — arguing that legacy 2D cameras are inherently weak in these conditions because they "measure appearance rather than physical state, relying heavily on color, texture, and contrast which break under environmental variability," and positions its depth-based 3D approach as "unaffected by shadows or color changes" and functional "in rain, strong sunlight, or low light." This is effectively a competitive/critical claim about 2D camera vendors' weak points, made by a 3D-camera vendor, rather than a neutral third-party assessment. — [LIPS: The 3D Revolution in Parking](https://www.lips-hci.com/post/the-3d-revolution-in-parking-why-precision-occupancy-is-a-measurement-problem-not-just-a-vision-task)

### Inferences
- There is an implicit industry debate visible in the sourced marketing content: 2D-camera vendors (Cleverciti, Park Assist, Milesight) address weather primarily through **hardware ruggedization** (IP ratings, heaters, vandal-resistant housings) and general AI/edge-processing claims, while depth-camera vendors (LIPS) argue that hardware ruggedization alone doesn't solve the underlying **algorithmic** weakness of appearance-based (2D/RGB) classification under variable lighting/shadow/occlusion conditions, and that only measuring physical depth/volume solves this class of error. Neither side publishes quantified, independently verified accuracy-by-condition data to settle this claim.
- No vendor researched published a specific "accuracy dropped from X% to Y% during snow cover" or "accuracy during >50% occlusion" style transparency metric — this level of granular, adverse-condition-specific performance disclosure appears to be absent industry-wide in public marketing materials, as opposed to being a differentiator any single vendor has published.

### Gaps
- No named, dated case study (e.g., "City X deployed Vendor Y and measured Z% accuracy during winter 2025") was found for any vendor — all weather/occlusion claims found are generic marketing assertions rather than case-study-backed with specific before/after or condition-segmented data.
- Could not access Cleverciti's official spec-sheet PDF (fetch returned corrupted/unreadable binary content) which may have contained more granular weather-condition performance data than what was available via general web page text.
- Trade press sources (Traffic Technology Today, Smart Cities World, ParkingNetwork, IPI) suggested in the assignment brief were not directly searched within this research pass due to time/tool-call constraints; these outlets may carry more specific occlusion/weather case-study data than vendor self-published marketing pages and would be a good follow-up source.
